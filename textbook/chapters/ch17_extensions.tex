\chapter{Extensions Beyond Binary Classification}
\label{ch:extensions}

The PAC theory of \Cref{ch:pac} and its characterization through VC dimension
apply to binary classification: the label set is $\{0,1\}$, the hypothesis
class is $\mathcal{H} \subseteq \{0,1\}^X$, and the loss is $0$-$1$.  This is
the setting where the theory is cleanest and most complete.  But most learning
problems are not binary classification.  Labels may come from a finite set of
$k > 2$ classes; the target may be a real-valued function; the data may be
noisy; the realizability assumption may fail.

This chapter asks: how does the binary theory extend to these richer settings?
The answer is not ``straightforwardly.''  Each extension requires
\emph{new mathematical primitives}: new notions of shattering, new complexity
measures, new assumptions.  The binary theory generalizes by \emph{grammar
growth}: substituting richer labels into the old definitions is never enough,
because each setting forces a primitive the old language cannot name.  In the
concept graph,
this is captured by edges of type \rel{extends\_grammar}: the source dimension
extends the grammar of the VC dimension by introducing primitives that have no
analogue in the binary case.

\medskip

The chapter has six sections.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Multiclass learning} (\Cref{sec:multiclass-ext}): the Natarajan
  dimension, the DS dimension, and the resolution of a thirty-year open problem.
\item \textbf{Real-valued functions} (\Cref{sec:real-valued}):
  pseudodimension and fat-shattering dimension.
\item \textbf{Agnostic learning} (\Cref{sec:agnostic-ext}): dropping realizability
  and the agnostic fundamental theorem.
\item \textbf{Noise-tolerant and partial concept learning}
  (\Cref{sec:noise-partial}): classification noise, malicious noise,
  and promise problems.
\item \textbf{Proper versus improper learning}
  (\Cref{sec:proper-improper}): the computational gap.
\item \textbf{The \rel{extends\_grammar} pattern}
  (\Cref{sec:extends-grammar}): what the extensions have in common.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Multiclass Learning: From Natarajan to DS}
\label{sec:multiclass-ext}

Let $Y = [k] = \{1, 2, \ldots, k\}$ for some $k \geq 2$, and let
$\mathcal{H} \subseteq Y^X$ be a multiclass hypothesis class.  The question
is: what characterizes PAC learnability of $\mathcal{H}$?

For $k = 2$, the answer is the VC dimension.  For $k > 2$, the answer was only
fully resolved in 2022, after a thirty-year journey through two competing
dimensions, each introducing a new shattering primitive.  That journey, and its
resolution, is the narrative backbone of this section.


\subsection{The Natarajan Dimension}

Natarajan~\cite{Natarajan1989} introduced the first multiclass complexity
measure by generalizing shattering to require two functions instead of one.

\begin{defn}[Natarajan Dimension {\cite{Natarajan1989}}]
\label{def:natarajan-dim}
The \emph{Natarajan dimension} $\Ndim(\mathcal{H})$ of a multiclass hypothesis
class $\mathcal{H} \subseteq Y^X$ is the largest size $d$ of a set
$\{x_1, \ldots, x_d\} \subseteq X$ for which there exist two functions
$f_0, f_1 \colon \{x_1, \ldots, x_d\} \to Y$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item $f_0(x_i) \neq f_1(x_i)$ for all $i \in [d]$, and
\item for every binary string $b \in \{0,1\}^d$, there exists $h \in
  \mathcal{H}$ with $h(x_i) = f_{b_i}(x_i)$ for all $i$.\formal{FLT_Proofs.Complexity.Structures.NatarajanDim}
\end{enumerate}
\end{defn}

The new primitive is the \emph{pair of functions} $(f_0, f_1)$.  In binary
classification, $f_0$ and $f_1$ are forced to be the constant functions $0$
and $1$, and the definition reduces to VC shattering.  In the multiclass case,
the choice of which two labels to use at each point is part of the
combinatorial structure.

\begin{example}[Natarajan shattering with $k=3$]
\label{ex:natarajan-k3}
Let $X = \{a, b\}$, $Y = \{1,2,3\}$.  Define $f_0(a)=1$, $f_0(b)=2$
and $f_1(a)=3$, $f_1(b)=1$.  To N-shatter $\{a,b\}$, we need four
hypotheses realizing every binary string:
\[
  h_{00}(a)=1,\, h_{00}(b)=2;\quad
  h_{01}(a)=1,\, h_{01}(b)=1;\quad
  h_{10}(a)=3,\, h_{10}(b)=2;\quad
  h_{11}(a)=3,\, h_{11}(b)=1.
\]
Any $\mathcal{H} \supseteq \{h_{00}, h_{01}, h_{10}, h_{11}\}$ has $\Ndim \geq 2$.
\end{example}

\begin{historical}
\textbf{The Natarajan gap (1989--2022).}
Natarajan showed that $\Ndim(\mathcal{H}) < \infty$ is \emph{sufficient}
for PAC learnability and provided sample complexity bounds.  The best
known bounds, due to Ben-David et al.~\cite{BenDavidCesaBianchiHausslerLong1995},
were:
\[
  C_1 \cdot \frac{\Ndim}{\varepsilon}
  \;\leq\; m_{\mathrm{PAC}}(\varepsilon,\delta)
  \;\leq\; C_2 \cdot \frac{\Ndim \cdot \ln k \cdot \ln(1/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}.
\]
Two gaps persisted.  First, the $\Theta(\log k)$ factor between upper
and lower bounds.  Second, and more fundamentally: is finiteness of $\Ndim$
also \emph{necessary}?  For finite label sets $|Y| = k < \infty$, the answer
is yes (the $\log k$ factor is at most a polynomial overhead).  But when
$|Y| = \infty$, a class can have $\Ndim = 1$ and yet fail to be PAC learnable.
This was the open problem that persisted for over thirty years.
\end{historical}


As a complexity measure, the \nde{natarajan\_dimension} extends the grammar of
the \nde{vc\_dimension}: the extension is the two-function shattering primitive
$(f_0, f_1)$.  The open frontier it leaves behind is a tight multiclass sample
complexity without the $\log k$ factor.


\subsection{The DS Dimension: A Thirty-Year Resolution}
\label{subsec:ds-dim}

The correct characterization of multiclass PAC learnability was established
by Brukhim et al.~\cite{BrukhimCarmonDinurMoranYehudayoff2022} using the
\emph{DS dimension}, named after Daniely and
Shalev-Shwartz~\cite{DanielyShalevShwartz2014} who introduced the
underlying concept of pseudo-cubes in one-inclusion hypergraphs.

\begin{defn}[One-Inclusion Hypergraph]
\label{def:oig}
Let $\mathcal{H} \subseteq Y^X$ and let $S = \{x_1, \ldots, x_n\} \subseteq X$
be a finite set.  The \emph{one-inclusion hypergraph} $\mathrm{OIG}(\mathcal{H}, S)$
has vertex set $\mathcal{H}|_S = \{(h(x_1), \ldots, h(x_n)) : h \in \mathcal{H}\}$
and, for each coordinate $i \in [n]$, a hyperedge connecting all vertices that
agree on all coordinates except possibly coordinate $i$.
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}[DS Dimension {\cite{DanielyShalevShwartz2014,BrukhimCarmonDinurMoranYehudayoff2022}}]
\label{def:ds-dim-ext}
A set $\{x_1, \ldots, x_d\} \subseteq X$ is \emph{DS-shattered} by
$\mathcal{H} \subseteq Y^X$ if there exists a function
$f \colon \{x_1, \ldots, x_d\} \to Y$ such that for every $i \in [d]$,
there exists $h_i \in \mathcal{H}$ with:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item $h_i(x_i) \neq f(x_i)$, and
\item $h_i(x_j) = f(x_j)$ for all $j \neq i$.
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{DS dimension} $\DSdim(\mathcal{H})$ is the size of the largest
DS-shattered set.
\end{defn}

The structural content of DS-shattering is different from Natarajan shattering
in a revealing way.  Natarajan shattering asks for a \emph{global} pair of
labeling functions with \emph{all} $2^d$ interpolations realized.
DS-shattering asks for a single ``default'' labeling $f$ and, for each
point, a \emph{local} witness that deviates at exactly that point.  The
requirement is weaker per point (only one deviation, not arbitrary binary
combinations) but more structural: it encodes a property of the
one-inclusion hypergraph of $\mathcal{H}$.

\begin{thm}[Multiclass Characterization {\cite{BrukhimCarmonDinurMoranYehudayoff2022}}]
\label{thm:multiclass-char}
A multiclass hypothesis class $\mathcal{H} \subseteq Y^X$ is PAC learnable if
and only if $\DSdim(\mathcal{H}) < \infty$.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
We outline both directions.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Sufficiency} ($\DSdim(\mathcal{H}) < \infty \Rightarrow$ learnable).
The proof proceeds through the one-inclusion hypergraph.  For a finite
restriction $\mathcal{H}|_S$ to a sample $S$ of size $m$, the one-inclusion
hypergraph $\mathrm{OIG}(\mathcal{H}, S)$ has vertices $\mathcal{H}|_S$ and
hyperedges indexed by coordinates.  The key insight of Daniely and
Shalev-Shwartz is that a \emph{proper orientation} of this hypergraph, an
assignment of each hyperedge to one of its vertices, yields a learning
algorithm.  Specifically:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item Given a sample $S = \{(x_1, y_1), \ldots, (x_m, y_m)\}$, restrict
  $\mathcal{H}$ to $\{x_1, \ldots, x_m\}$ and build $\mathrm{OIG}(\mathcal{H}, S)$.
\item Find a proper orientation with \emph{minimum outdegree}.
\item Return the hypothesis whose vertex has minimum outdegree in the
  orientation.
\end{enumerate}

If $\DSdim(\mathcal{H}) = d < \infty$, then the one-inclusion hypergraph
of any restriction has no $d$-dimensional pseudo-cube, and a combinatorial
argument (via the Helly property of CAT(0) cube complexes) shows that
a proper orientation with outdegree at most $O(d)$ exists.  The learner's
error is bounded by $O(d/m)$, yielding PAC learnability with sample
complexity $O(d/\varepsilon)$.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Necessity} ($\DSdim(\mathcal{H}) = \infty \Rightarrow$ not learnable).
If $\DSdim(\mathcal{H}) = \infty$, then for every $d$, there exists a
finite set that is DS-shattered.  The proof constructs, for each $d$, a
distribution $D_d$ over $X \times Y$ such that:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item The Bayes-optimal classifier has zero error.
\item Any learner given $m < d$ samples has expected error
  $\Omega(d/m)$ on $D_d$.
\end{itemize}
The distribution is supported on the DS-shattered set and uses the default
labeling $f$ as the target.  The witness hypotheses $h_1, \ldots, h_d$ act
as ``confusers'': a learner that has not seen point $x_i$ cannot distinguish
$f$ from $h_i$, because they agree everywhere except at $x_i$.  Since this
works for arbitrarily large $d$, no finite sample suffices for
$\varepsilon$-accurate learning uniformly over $D_d$.
\end{proof}


\begin{obstbox}
\textbf{Natarajan $\not\Rightarrow$ PAC learnability when $|Y| = \infty$.}

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Witness (Brukhim et al.~2022).}
There exists a hypothesis class $\mathcal{H} \subseteq Y^X$ with
$\Ndim(\mathcal{H}) = 1$ but $\DSdim(\mathcal{H}) = \infty$.  The
construction uses a hyperbolic pseudo-manifold: the domain $X$ is the
vertex set of a high-dimensional simplicial complex with hyperbolic
geometry, and $\mathcal{H}$ encodes colorings of simplices.  The
hyperbolic geometry ensures that any two points can be N-shattered
(only two labels are needed locally), but the global structure admits
arbitrarily large DS-shattered sets because the one-inclusion hypergraph
contains arbitrarily large pseudo-cubes.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Structural lesson.}
The Natarajan dimension is a \emph{local} measure: it examines pairs of
labels at each point.  The DS dimension is a \emph{global} measure: it
examines the connectivity structure of the entire one-inclusion hypergraph.
When $|Y| = \infty$, local two-label constraints do not capture global
learnability.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Graph edge.}  Finiteness of the \nde{natarajan\_dimension} does not imply
\nde{pac\_learning}, witnessed by the Brukhim et al.\ (2022) non-learnable class
with $\Ndim = 1$.
\end{obstbox}


The relationship between the two dimensions is:

\begin{prop}[DS vs.\ Natarajan]
\label{prop:ds-vs-nat}
For any $\mathcal{H} \subseteq Y^X$:
\[
  \Ndim(\mathcal{H}) \;\leq\; \DSdim(\mathcal{H}).
\]
Moreover, when $|Y| = k < \infty$:
\[
  \DSdim(\mathcal{H}) \;\leq\; O\!\bigl(\Ndim(\mathcal{H}) \cdot \log k\bigr).
\]
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
For the first inequality, we show that every N-shattered set is also
DS-shattered.  Suppose $S = \{x_1, \ldots, x_d\}$ is N-shattered by a pair
$(f_0, f_1)$.  Take the default labeling $f = f_0$.  For each $i$, the binary
string $e_i$ (with a $1$ in coordinate $i$ and $0$ elsewhere) is realized by
some $h_i \in \mathcal{H}$ with $h_i(x_i) = f_1(x_i) \neq f_0(x_i) = f(x_i)$
and $h_i(x_j) = f_0(x_j) = f(x_j)$ for $j \neq i$.  Thus $f$ together with
$h_1, \ldots, h_d$ witnesses that $S$ is DS-shattered.  Every N-shattered set
is therefore DS-shattered, so $\Ndim \leq \DSdim$.  DS-shattering is the
\emph{weaker} requirement (only $d$ single-coordinate deviations, not all
$2^d$ interpolations), which is exactly why $\DSdim$ can be infinite while
$\Ndim$ stays finite, as in the obstruction above.

For the second inequality (finite $k$), suppose $\DSdim = D$.  Any
DS-shattered set provides $D$ witness hypotheses deviating from a default.
Since each deviation chooses one of $k-1$ alternative labels, a pigeonhole
argument over the $k^D$ possible deviation patterns shows
$D \leq O(\Ndim \cdot \log k)$.
\end{proof}


As a complexity measure, the \nde{ds\_dimension} characterizes \nde{pac\_learning}
in the multiclass setting, and it is strictly stronger than the
\nde{natarajan\_dimension}, witnessed by the hyperbolic pseudo-manifold
construction.  This is the measure that resolved a thirty-year open problem in
the theory.

\section{Real-Valued Functions}
\label{sec:real-valued}

Now let $\mathcal{F} \subseteq \R^X$ be a class of real-valued functions.
The learner observes samples $(x_i, y_i)$ with $y_i = f(x_i)$ (or
$y_i \approx f(x_i)$ in the noisy case) and aims to approximate $f$.
The $0$-$1$ loss is replaced by the squared loss or absolute loss, and the
question becomes: what is the analogue of VC dimension?

Two dimensions extend the binary theory.  Both introduce \emph{thresholds} as
a new primitive.


\subsection{Pseudodimension}

\begin{defn}[Pseudodimension {\cite{Pollard1984,Haussler1992}}]
\label{def:pseudodim-ext}
The \emph{pseudodimension} $\Pdim(\mathcal{F})$ of a function class
$\mathcal{F} \subseteq \R^X$ is the VC dimension of the class of subgraphs:
\[
  \Pdim(\mathcal{F}) \;=\; \VC\!\bigl(\bigl\{
    x \mapsto \ind[f(x) \geq t] : f \in \mathcal{F},\; t \in \R
  \bigr\}\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, $\Pdim(\mathcal{F}) = d$ if there exist $x_1, \ldots, x_d \in X$
and thresholds $t_1, \ldots, t_d \in \R$ such that for every
$b \in \{0,1\}^d$, there exists $f \in \mathcal{F}$ with
$f(x_i) \geq t_i \iff b_i = 1$.\formal{FLT_Proofs.Complexity.Structures.Pseudodimension}
\end{defn}

The new primitive is the \emph{threshold vector} $(t_1, \ldots, t_d)$.
In binary classification, thresholds are unnecessary: the outputs are
already $0$ or $1$.  For real-valued functions, thresholds convert the
continuous outputs into a binary shattering condition.

\begin{example}[Linear functions]
For the class of linear functions $\mathcal{F} = \{x \mapsto w^\top x : w \in \R^d\}$
on $\R^d$, we have $\Pdim(\mathcal{F}) = d$.  Any $d$ affinely independent
points $x_1, \ldots, x_d$ can be P-shattered with thresholds $t_i = 0$:
for each $b \in \{0,1\}^d$, one can find $w$ such that $\mathrm{sign}(w^\top x_i) = (-1)^{1-b_i}$.
\end{example}

\begin{prop}[Pseudodimension and covering numbers]
\label{prop:pdim-covering}
If $\Pdim(\mathcal{F}) = d < \infty$, then for every probability measure
$P$ on $X$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$:
\[
  \mathcal{N}(\varepsilon, \mathcal{F}, L_1(P))
  \;\leq\; \left(\frac{2e}{\varepsilon}\right)^d.
\]
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The subgraph class $\{x \mapsto \ind[f(x) \geq t] : f \in \mathcal{F},\,
t \in \R\}$ has VC dimension $\Pdim(\mathcal{F}) = d$ by definition.  Applying
the Sauer--Shelah bound and the standard covering-number estimate for VC
classes (\Cref{ch:generalization}) to this binary class, then transferring
back to $L_1(P)$ through the layer-cake identity
$f = \int_0^1 \ind[f \geq t]\,\mathrm{d}t$, yields the stated bound.
\end{proof}

As a complexity measure, the \nde{pseudodimension} extends the grammar of the
\nde{vc\_dimension}, and the extension is the threshold primitive.


\subsection{Fat-Shattering Dimension}

Pseudodimension controls learnability in the realizable case.  For the
agnostic setting with real-valued targets, a scale-sensitive
notion is needed.

\begin{defn}[Fat-Shattering Dimension {\cite{AlonBenDavidCesaBianchiHaussler1997}}]
\label{def:fat-shattering-ext}
For $\gamma > 0$, the \emph{$\gamma$-fat-shattering dimension}
$\fatshat_\gamma(\mathcal{F})$ is the largest $d$ such that there exist
$x_1, \ldots, x_d \in X$ and thresholds $t_1, \ldots, t_d \in \R$ satisfying:
for every $b \in \{0,1\}^d$, there exists $f \in \mathcal{F}$ with
\[
  f(x_i) \geq t_i + \gamma \;\text{if } b_i = 1,
  \qquad
  f(x_i) \leq t_i - \gamma \;\text{if } b_i = 0.
\]
\end{defn}

The new primitive beyond pseudodimension is the \emph{margin}
$\gamma$.\formal{FLT_Proofs.Complexity.Structures.FatShatteringDim}
As $\gamma \to 0$, the fat-shattering dimension approaches the
pseudodimension.

\begin{thm}[Real-Valued Characterization {\cite{AlonBenDavidCesaBianchiHaussler1997}}]
\label{thm:real-valued-char}
A class $\mathcal{F} \subseteq [0,1]^X$ of bounded real-valued functions is
agnostically PAC learnable with respect to the absolute loss if and only if
$\fatshat_\gamma(\mathcal{F}) < \infty$ for all $\gamma > 0$.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\textbf{Sufficiency.}  If $\fatshat_\gamma(\mathcal{F}) = d_\gamma < \infty$
for all $\gamma > 0$, then the $L_2$ covering numbers satisfy
$\log \mathcal{N}_2(\varepsilon, \mathcal{F}, x_1^n) \leq
O(d_{\varepsilon/4} \cdot \log^2(n/d_{\varepsilon/4}))$.
Finite covering numbers imply uniform convergence of empirical risk to
true risk, which yields agnostic PAC learnability.

\textbf{Necessity.}  If $\fatshat_\gamma(\mathcal{F}) = \infty$ for some
$\gamma > 0$, then for every $n$, one can $\gamma$-fat-shatter $n$ points.
By a probabilistic argument, any empirical risk minimizer on a sample of
size $m$ has expected error $\Omega(\gamma)$ on a suitably chosen
distribution, regardless of $m$.  Hence $\mathcal{F}$ is not learnable
at accuracy $\gamma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scale families versus single numbers]
\label{rmk:scale-family}
The fat-shattering characterization is a \emph{family} of finiteness
requirements, one for each $\gamma > 0$, where a single threshold-free number
would have sufficed in the binary case.  This is
another instance of grammar growth: the real-valued setting requires a
scale parameter that has no analogue in the binary theory.  In practice,
$\fatshat_\gamma$ is often a decreasing function of $\gamma$; larger
margins are harder to maintain, so fewer points can be fat-shattered.
\end{remark}

\section{Agnostic Learning: Dropping Realizability}
\label{sec:agnostic-ext}

All results so far have assumed \emph{realizability}: there exists some
$h^* \in \mathcal{H}$ with $\risk_D(h^*) = 0$.  In practice, models are
always wrong; the question is how wrong.  The agnostic setting, introduced
by Haussler~\cite{Haussler1992}, removes the realizability assumption entirely.

\begin{defn}[Agnostic PAC Learning {\cite{Haussler1992}}]
\label{def:agnostic-pac-ext}
A hypothesis class $\mathcal{H}$ is \emph{agnostically PAC learnable}
if there exists a learner $A$ and a function $m \colon (0,1)^2 \to \N$ such
that for every distribution $D$ over $X \times \{0,1\}$ (with \emph{no}
assumption that the Bayes-optimal classifier lies in $\mathcal{H}$), for
every $\varepsilon, \delta > 0$: given $m(\varepsilon, \delta)$ i.i.d.\
samples from $D$,
\[
  \Pr_{S \sim D^m}\!\Bigl[
    \risk_D\!\bigl(A(S)\bigr)
    \;\leq\;
    \inf_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \risk_D(h) + \varepsilon
  \Bigr] \;\geq\; 1 - \delta.
\]
\end{defn}

The learner must compete with the \emph{best hypothesis in the class},
not with the Bayes-optimal classifier.  The gap
$\inf_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \risk_D(h)$ may be positive; the learner need
only close to within $\varepsilon$ of it.

\begin{thm}[Agnostic Fundamental Theorem]
\label{thm:agnostic-ft}
For binary classification with $0$-$1$ loss, a hypothesis class
$\mathcal{H} \subseteq \{0,1\}^X$ is agnostically PAC learnable if and only
if $\VC(\mathcal{H}) < \infty$.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The characterizing condition is the same as in the realizable case, finite
VC dimension, but the proof mechanism and sample complexity differ.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Sufficiency.}  Suppose $\VC(\mathcal{H}) = d < \infty$.  The learner
runs empirical risk minimization: $A(S) = \arg\min_{h \in \mathcal{H}}
\emprisk_S(h)$.  By the uniform convergence theorem for VC classes, with
probability at least $1 - \delta$ over a sample of size
$m = O\!\bigl(\frac{d + \log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon^2}\bigr)$,
\[
  \sup_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \bigl|\risk_D(h) - \emprisk_S(h)\bigr|
  \;\leq\; \varepsilon/2.
\]
Let $h^* = \arg\min_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \risk_D(h)$ and $\hat{h} = A(S)$.
Then:
\begin{align*}
  \risk_D(\hat{h})
  &\leq \emprisk_S(\hat{h}) + \varepsilon/2
    &\text{(uniform convergence)}\\
  &\leq \emprisk_S(h^*) + \varepsilon/2
    &\text{(ERM chose $\hat{h}$)}\\
  &\leq \risk_D(h^*) + \varepsilon/2 + \varepsilon/2
    &\text{(uniform convergence)}\\
  &= \risk_D(h^*) + \varepsilon.
\end{align*}

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Necessity.}  If $\VC(\mathcal{H}) = \infty$, the No-Free-Lunch
argument of the realizable case applies \textit{a fortiori}: for any learner
and any sample size $m$, there exists a realizable distribution (which is
a special case of an arbitrary distribution) on which the learner fails.
\end{proof}

\begin{separation}
\textbf{Agnostic versus realizable sample complexity.}

\medskip\noindent
The agnostic and realizable settings are characterized by the \emph{same}
condition ($\VC < \infty$), but the sample complexities differ:
\[
  m_{\mathrm{realizable}}(\varepsilon, \delta)
  = \Theta\!\left(\frac{d}{\varepsilon} + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon}\right),
  \qquad
  m_{\mathrm{agnostic}}(\varepsilon, \delta)
  = \Theta\!\left(\frac{d}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon^2}\right).
\]
The quadratic dependence on $1/\varepsilon$ in the agnostic case is inherent: a
matching lower bound forces it, so no sharper analysis can remove it.  The bound is achieved by
distributions where the optimal hypothesis has error $1/2 - \varepsilon$,
and distinguishing it from a hypothesis with error $1/2$ requires
$\Omega(1/\varepsilon^2)$ samples by a Chernoff-bound argument.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Graph edge.}  \nde{agnostic\_pac\_learning} restricts \nde{pac\_learning}
in the graph: agnostic PAC learning \emph{generalizes} standard PAC learning by
removing the realizability constraint.
\end{separation}

\section{Noise-Tolerant and Partial Concept Learning}
\label{sec:noise-partial}

\subsection{Classification Noise}

The \emph{classification noise} model (Angluin and
Laird~\cite{AngluinLaird1988}) modifies PAC learning by flipping each
label independently with probability $\eta < 1/2$: the learner observes
$(x, y \oplus z)$ where $z \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\eta)$ and $y$ is the true
label.  The question is whether finite VC dimension still suffices for
learning.

\begin{thm}[Noise-tolerant PAC learning]
\label{thm:noise-tolerant}
If $\VC(\mathcal{H}) = d < \infty$ and the noise rate $\eta < 1/2$ is known,
then $\mathcal{H}$ is PAC learnable under classification noise with sample
complexity
\[
  m = O\!\left(\frac{d}{(1-2\eta)^2 \varepsilon^2}
  + \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{(1-2\eta)^2 \varepsilon^2}\right).
\]
\end{thm}

The bound is an upper bound, achieved by a noise-robust empirical risk
minimizer~\cite{AngluinLaird1988}; the $(1-2\eta)^{-2}$ dependence is also
necessary, so the rate is tight.  The sample complexity degrades as
$\eta \to 1/2$, where the noise rate approaches the information-theoretic
limit at which labels carry no signal.

\begin{remark}[SQ connection]
\label{rmk:sq-noise}
Statistical query (SQ) learnable classes are automatically noise-tolerant:
an SQ algorithm estimates expectations $\E_D[\phi(x, y)]$ to tolerance
$\tau$, and these can be simulated from noisy samples with a $1/(1-2\eta)$
overhead in sample size.  However, not all noise-tolerant classes are
SQ learnable: parities are PAC learnable (via Gaussian elimination) and
hence noise-tolerant for known $\eta$, but not SQ learnable
($\SQdim = 2^n$).
\end{remark}


\subsection{Partial Concept Learning}

In \emph{partial concept learning} (also called \emph{promise problems}),
the hypothesis class $\mathcal{H}$ is defined only on a subset
$P \subseteq X$, the \emph{promise set}.  Points outside $P$ have no
defined label, and the distribution $D$ is supported on $P$.

\begin{defn}[Partial Concept Class]
\label{def:partial-concept}
A \emph{partial concept class} is a set $\mathcal{H} \subseteq \{0, 1, *\}^X$
where $*$ denotes ``undefined.''  A distribution $D$ is \emph{compatible}
with $h \in \mathcal{H}$ if $D$ is supported on $\{x : h(x) \neq *\}$.
\end{defn}

Partial concepts arise naturally in many settings: medical diagnosis where
only symptomatic patients have definite diagnoses, or feature spaces where
certain regions are ``don't care'' zones.

Alon, Hanneke, Holzman, and Moran~\cite{AlonHannekeHolzmanMoran2021}
developed the PAC theory of partial concept classes, and the picture is
genuinely new.  A partial class is PAC learnable if and only if its
(partial) VC dimension is finite, where shattering is restricted to the
promise region; so far, the binary analogue carries over.  What does
\emph{not} carry over is the reduction to total concepts: they exhibit
partial classes of VC dimension~$1$ that admit \emph{no} total extension of
finite VC dimension, and that cannot be learned by empirical risk
minimization at all.  Partial-concept learning is therefore not a special
case of the total theory; it is a setting where proper and ERM-based
learning provably fail while improper learning succeeds, and where the
computational landscape shifts: problems easy as total concepts can become
hard under a promise constraint.

\section{Proper Versus Improper Learning}
\label{sec:proper-improper}

All PAC definitions permit the learner to output \emph{any} efficiently
evaluatable hypothesis, not just one from the target class $\mathcal{H}$.
When the learner is restricted to output a hypothesis from $\mathcal{H}$
itself, we call it a \emph{proper} learner.

\begin{defn}[Proper and Improper PAC Learning]
\label{def:proper-improper-ext}
A PAC learner $A$ for class $\mathcal{H}$ is \emph{proper} if
$A(S) \in \mathcal{H}$ for all samples $S$.  It is \emph{improper} if
$A(S)$ may lie in a larger class $\mathcal{H}' \supseteq \mathcal{H}$.
\end{defn}

\begin{remark}
In the information-theoretic (sample complexity) sense, there is no
separation between proper and improper learning for standard PAC: both are
characterized by finite VC dimension, and the sample complexities are
$\Theta(d/\varepsilon)$ in both cases.  The separation is \emph{computational}.
\end{remark}

\begin{thm}[Pitt--Valiant {\cite{PittValiant1988}}]
\label{thm:pitt-valiant}
For $k \geq 2$, $k$-term DNF is not properly PAC learnable unless $\mathrm{RP} = \mathrm{NP}$.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
The proof reduces graph $k$-colorability to the problem of finding a
consistent $k$-term DNF formula.

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a graph with $V = \{v_1, \ldots, v_n\}$.  We construct
a learning instance over $X = \{0,1\}^n$ as follows.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Positive examples.}  For each vertex $v_i$, create the example
$e_i^+ \in \{0,1\}^n$ defined by $e_i^+(j) = 1$ if $j \neq i$ and
$e_i^+(i) = 0$.  Label: positive.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Negative examples.}  For each edge $(v_i, v_j) \in E$, create the
example $e_{ij}^- \in \{0,1\}^n$ defined by $e_{ij}^-(l) = 1$ if
$l \notin \{i, j\}$, and $e_{ij}^-(i) = e_{ij}^-(j) = 0$.  Label: negative.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Claim.}  A consistent $k$-term DNF exists for this labeled sample
if and only if $G$ is $k$-colorable.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Forward direction.}  Given a proper $k$-coloring $c \colon V \to [k]$,
define the $k$-term DNF $\phi = T_1 \vee \cdots \vee T_k$ where
$T_j = \bigwedge_{i : c(v_i) = j} \overline{x_i}$.  Each positive example
$e_i^+$ satisfies $T_{c(v_i)}$ (since $e_i^+(i) = 0$ makes the literal
$\overline{x_i}$ true, and all other literals in $T_{c(v_i)}$ are true
because $e_i^+(l) = 1$ for $l \neq i$).  Each negative example $e_{ij}^-$
falsifies every term: for any term $T_j$, since $(v_i, v_j)$ is an edge
and $c$ is proper, $c(v_i) \neq c(v_j)$, so at most one of $v_i, v_j$
is in color class $j$, and the other has $e_{ij}^-(l) = 0$ which falsifies
a literal not in $T_j$.  (A careful case analysis confirms the argument.)

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Reverse direction.}  Given a consistent $k$-term DNF
$\phi = T_1 \vee \cdots \vee T_k$, assign color $c(v_i) = \min\{j : e_i^+
\text{ satisfies } T_j\}$.  Since $e_i^+$ is positive, at least one term is
satisfied.  For any edge $(v_i, v_j)$, the negative example $e_{ij}^-$
falsifies $\phi$, which forces $c(v_i) \neq c(v_j)$ (otherwise the term
satisfied by both would also satisfy $e_{ij}^-$).  Hence $c$ is a proper
$k$-coloring.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Conclusion.}  A proper PAC learner for $k$-term DNF, given the
above examples (which can be generated efficiently from $G$), would find
a consistent $k$-term DNF in polynomial time, thereby solving $k$-colorability
in polynomial time.  Since $k$-colorability is NP-complete for $k \geq 3$
(and NP-hard to decide in randomized polynomial time unless
$\mathrm{RP} = \mathrm{NP}$), proper PAC learning of $k$-term DNF is
computationally hard.
\end{proof}


\begin{separation}
\textbf{Proper $\not\Rightarrow$ efficient learning: the DNF witness.}

\medskip\noindent
The same class that is hard to learn properly is easy to learn improperly.
Observe that every $k$-term DNF over $n$ variables is a $k$-CNF formula
(by distributing), and $k$-CNF is learnable in polynomial time: simply
enumerate all $O(n^k)$ candidate clauses and filter by consistency with the
data.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Summary.} For $k = 3$: 3-term DNF is NP-hard proper, polynomial-time
improper (via 3-CNF).  This is the cleanest known computational separation
in PAC learning.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Graph edge.}  The \nde{proper\_improper\_separation} does not imply
\nde{pac\_learning}, witnessed by $3$-term DNF: NP-hard proper, polynomial-time
improper.
\end{separation}

\section{The \rel{extends\_grammar} Pattern}
\label{sec:extends-grammar}

The extensions of the previous sections share a common structure.  Each
begins with the VC-dimension framework and introduces new primitives that
have no analogue in binary classification:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llp{5.5cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Extension} & \textbf{New Primitive} & \textbf{Why Needed} \\
\midrule
Natarajan dim. & Two-function shattering $(f_0, f_1)$
  & With $k > 2$ labels, a single bit string does not
    describe a labeling. \\[4pt]
DS dimension & One-inclusion witness
  & Global hypergraph structure governs learnability
    when $|Y| = \infty$. \\[4pt]
Pseudodimension & Threshold vector $(t_1, \ldots, t_d)$
  & Real-valued outputs must be discretized before
    shattering can be defined. \\[4pt]
Fat-shattering dim. & Scale parameter $\gamma$
  & Agnostic real-valued learning requires margin;
    finiteness at all scales replaces a single
    finiteness condition. \\[4pt]
Agnostic PAC & Benchmark $\inf_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \risk(h)$
  & Without realizability, success is measured relative
    to the best in class. \\[4pt]
Noise model & Noise rate $\eta$
  & Labels are corrupted; sample complexity depends on
    the signal-to-noise ratio $(1-2\eta)^{-2}$. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

In each case, the extension is an \rel{extends\_grammar} edge in the concept
graph rather than a \rel{restricts} edge.  The distinction matters:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item A \rel{restricts} edge means the target is a special case of the source
  (no new vocabulary needed).
\item An \rel{extends\_grammar} edge means the source introduces
  \emph{new vocabulary}: concepts that cannot be expressed in the target's
  language.  The extension is irreducible.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[The non-reduction principle]
\label{rmk:non-reduction}
A tempting strategy for multiclass or real-valued learning is to reduce to
binary classification (e.g., one-vs-all for multiclass, or
thresholding for real-valued).  Such reductions are computationally useful but
do not eliminate the need for new dimensions.  The VC dimension of the
reduced class does not generally equal the Natarajan, DS, pseudo-, or
fat-shattering dimension of the original class.  The reductions introduce
approximation gaps that the native dimensions avoid.  This is why the
\rel{extends\_grammar} edges exist: they mark the points where reduction to the
binary theory loses information.
\end{remark}

The grammar growth \emph{is} the extension.  For the multiclass case this
necessity is a theorem: the Natarajan--DS gap, the class with $\Ndim = 1$
and $\DSdim = \infty$, shows that no two-function dimension can characterize
learnability when $|Y| = \infty$.  For the real-valued, agnostic, and noisy
settings the necessity is the working understanding rather than a proven
impossibility (no simpler characterization is known), but the pattern is the
same: each new primitive expresses what the existing one cannot.


\subsection{Where Transformers Live}
\label{sec:transformer-grammar}

The extensions of this chapter are not abstractions a practitioner can avoid;
a transformer inhabits several at once.  Its next-token head is a
\emph{multiclass} hypothesis class over the vocabulary (tens of thousands of
labels, and unbounded in the limit of growing vocabularies), so it sits in
exactly the regime where the Natarajan dimension fails and the DS dimension is
the right measure.  Its logits are \emph{real-valued}, so its capacity is
governed by a scale-sensitive dimension; the certified generalization bound of
\Cref{ch:generalization} is, in this language, a fat-shattering and
covering-number control of the attention head.  And training is
\emph{agnostic}: no realizable $h^\star$ is assumed.

What is machine-checked is the binary core.  A softmax-attention head induces
a concept class verified to be
well-behaved,\formal{TLT.Bridge.softAttention_wellBehaved} hence of finite VC
dimension in the binary setting.  Whether that control \emph{lifts} to the
multiclass next-token head, specifically whether the DS dimension of an attention class
over a large label set is finite, is open, and is the form the central
question of this chapter takes for transformers (\Cref{open:attention-ds}).

\section{Open Problems}
\label{sec:extensions-open}

Extending a characterization is where learning theory most often stumbles: a
dimension that works for binary classification need not, and the multiclass
history is the cautionary tale.  Each problem below is tagged a \emph{known
unknown} (KU), sharply posed but unresolved, or an \emph{unknown known} (UK),
where the right object or its formalization is still missing; a design-lab or
literature anchor is named where one exists.

\begin{openprob}[Tight multiclass sample complexity]
\label{open:multiclass-tight}
For finite $|Y| = k$, the best known bounds leave a $\Theta(\log k)$ gap
between the Natarajan-dimension-based upper and lower bounds on sample
complexity.  Does the DS dimension close this gap, yielding a sample
complexity of $\Theta(\DSdim / \varepsilon)$ without logarithmic factors?
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[Multiclass online learning]
\label{open:multiclass-online}
The DS dimension characterizes PAC learnability in the multiclass setting.
What is the correct characterization of \emph{online} multiclass learnability?
Is there a multiclass analogue of the Littlestone dimension, and does it
relate to the DS dimension as the Littlestone dimension relates to VC?
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[Formalizing the DS dimension]
\label{open:formalize-ds}
The Natarajan, pseudo-, and fat-shattering dimensions are machine-checked
(\leanname{NatarajanDim}, \leanname{Pseudodimension}, \leanname{FatShatteringDim}),
but the genuine Daniely--Shalev-Shwartz DS dimension is not: the kernel's
\leanname{StrongShatteringDim} is the \emph{all-labelings} dimension (every $f \colon S \to Y$
realizable), whose value is at most $\Ndim$, the \emph{opposite} of the
real DS relationship $\Ndim \le \DSdim$.  Can the true DS dimension, defined by default plus single-coordinate
deviations, and the multiclass characterization be
formalized~\cite{BrukhimCarmonDinurMoranYehudayoff2022}?  An unknown known.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[The DS dimension of attention]
\label{open:attention-ds}
A softmax-attention head is verified well-behaved, hence finite-VC, in the
binary setting (\leanname{softAttention_wellBehaved}).  Is the DS dimension of
the multiclass next-token attention class (over a vocabulary of size $k$, and
as $k \to \infty$) finite?  A known unknown on the transformer side, and the
multiclass form of this chapter's central question.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[The fat-shattering dimension of attention]
\label{open:attention-fat}
The certified generalization bound for attention (\Cref{ch:generalization}) is
a covering-number control of real-valued logits.  Does it match the
fat-shattering dimension (\leanname{FatShatteringDim}) of the attention logit
class, and is that dimension finite at every scale $\gamma$?  A known unknown on
the transformer side.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[A realizable characterization via pseudodimension]
\label{open:pdim-realizable}
\Cref{prop:pdim-covering} bounds covering numbers, and
\Cref{thm:real-valued-char} characterizes the \emph{agnostic} case via
fat-shattering.  Is finiteness of the pseudodimension (\leanname{Pseudodimension})
exactly the characterization of \emph{realizable} real-valued learnability, and
can it be proved as a clean biconditional?  A known unknown.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[Formalizing partial-concept learnability]
\label{open:partial-formal}
The partial VC dimension characterizes partial-concept PAC learnability, with
the surprise that learnable partial classes need not extend to finite-VC total
classes~\cite{AlonHannekeHolzmanMoran2021}.  Can this be formalized, including
the no-finite-extension witness?  An unknown known: the partial-concept model
is absent from the kernel.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[List learning]
\label{open:list-learning}
A list learner outputs a short list of labels and succeeds if the true label
is among them.  What combinatorial dimension characterizes list PAC
learnability, and how does it relate to the DS
dimension~\cite{BrukhimCarmonDinurMoranYehudayoff2022}?  A known unknown.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[Agnostic proper versus improper]
\label{open:agnostic-proper}
In the realizable case, proper and improper PAC have the same sample
complexity (\Cref{sec:proper-improper}).  In the \emph{agnostic} case, is there
a class for which proper learning needs strictly more samples than improper
learning, an information-theoretic rather than merely computational separation?  A
known unknown.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[Noise-tolerant real-valued learning]
\label{open:noise-fat}
Classification noise tolerance is understood for binary VC classes
(\Cref{thm:noise-tolerant}).  What is the fat-shattering analogue: how does
label noise on real-valued targets degrade the scale-sensitive sample
complexity?  A known unknown.
\end{openprob}

\subsection*{Counterfactual exercises: generalizing Figure~\ref{fig:ch17-concept-map}}

The concept map is a lattice with a single lit rung, and the exercises below test
where that one rung sits.  The drawing carries two independent codes.  Color names
the relationship type: \rel{extends\_grammar} in orange, \rel{strictly\_stronger}
in red, \rel{characterizes} in blue, \rel{restricts} in green, \rel{lower\_bounds}
in brown.  Line style names the evidence: solid where the kernel certifies a
theorem sorry-free, dashed where the result is established in the literature,
dotted where the edge is a conceptual grammar-extension or restriction.  Exactly
one edge is solid, the VC dimension characterizing binary PAC learning
(\leanname{fundamental_theorem}); the three literature characterizations, the
strictly-stronger edge, and the proper-improper lower bound are dashed; the four
grammar-extension and restriction edges are dotted.  Each exercise below reads the
concrete map as a special case of a wider claim: sometimes an \emph{instance} (an
edge, node, or sub-map already drawn), sometimes a \emph{boundary} (one code,
direction, or evidence grade toggled so an edge changes color, style, or
orientation), sometimes a \emph{frontier} (a dashed or dotted edge whose
formalization is the next move).  The asymmetry to keep in view: one rung is
verified and everything around it is borrowed.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{The one solid edge is the only rung the kernel has climbed.} Read
only the solid stroke and ignore the dashed and dotted ones. This is the
figure's defining verified instance: of the ten edges, exactly one is
machine-checked, the biconditional that VC dimension characterizes realizable
binary PAC learnability (\leanname{fundamental_theorem}, over $\mathcal{H}
\subseteq \{0,1\}^X$; the agnostic-binary characterization \Cref{thm:agnostic-ft}
is a separate literature result, drawn dashed at $vc \to agn$), so the solid
subgraph is the single edge $vc \to pac$ and everything above it in the grammar
is borrowed; the all-dashed-and-dotted periphery is precisely the reach the
kernel has not yet attained, which is why the figure is the abstract witness that
the extensions form a frontier with exactly one verified rung, the single edge
$vc \to pac$, while the tower above it still awaits a verifier.

\item \textbf{Grammar growth is the lesson the orange backbone encodes.} Read the
orange \rel{extends\_grammar} edges as a class and ignore node
identities. This orange subgraph is the instance that names the chapter's thesis
(\Cref{sec:extends-grammar}): each orange edge marks a place where the binary
language provably loses information and a new primitive must be added, the pair
$(f_0, f_1)$ for Natarajan, the threshold vector then the margin $\gamma$ for the
real-valued branch, the noise rate $\eta$ for the noise branch, so ``how does
binary PAC generalize?'' has the structural answer ``by adding irreducible
vocabulary,'' and the map is the witness that no reduction to the binary theory
(\Cref{rmk:non-reduction}) collapses these edges.

\item \textbf{Each extension's new primitive is an instance of one move.} Pick any
single orange edge and read its source. Each such edge is an instance that exhibits
the per-extension primitive: $nat \to vc$ adds two-function shattering
(\Cref{def:natarajan-dim}), $pdim \to vc$ adds the threshold vector
(\Cref{def:pseudodim-ext}), the fat-shattering refinement adds the scale $\gamma$
(\Cref{def:fat-shattering-ext}), and the new $noise \to pac$ edge adds $\eta$
(\Cref{thm:noise-tolerant}); each is one expansion of the binary grammar, and the
figure asserts that the expansions are instances of the same \rel{extends\_grammar}
relation rather than unrelated facts.

\item \textbf{A node can be formalized as a structure while its characterizing edge
stays literature.} Read the Natarajan, pseudodimension, and fat-shattering nodes
together with the edges leaving them. This pairing is the figure's principal evidence
boundary: the three dimension nodes are machine-checked as kernel structures
(\leanname{NatarajanDim}, \leanname{Pseudodimension}, \leanname{FatShatteringDim},
each a sorry-free definition), yet the edges that would make them
\emph{characterize} a learning setting, $ds \to pac$ and $fat \to pac$, are dashed
because the characterization theorems are literature
(\cite{BrukhimCarmonDinurMoranYehudayoff2022},
\cite{AlonBenDavidCesaBianchiHaussler1997}); so a node solid as a structure and
dashed as an edge is exactly the distinction the two-code scheme protects, and
drawing the edge solid because the node is formalized would overstate what the
kernel proves.

\item \textbf{DS dominates Natarajan, yet the kernel verifies only the other rung.}
Read the dashed red $ds \to nat$ edge and ask which inequality the kernel actually
holds. The answer marks a subtler evidence boundary: the strict domination $\Ndim
\le \DSdim$ with the $\Ndim = 1$, $\DSdim = \infty$ obstruction
(\Cref{prop:ds-vs-nat}, \cite{BrukhimCarmonDinurMoranYehudayoff2022}) is the
literature content of the edge, but the kernel proves the \emph{reverse}
inequality, strong-shattering dimension below Natarajan
(\leanname{strongShatteringDim_le_NatarajanDim}, sorry-free), because its
\leanname{StrongShatteringDim} is the all-labelings dimension, not the genuine
Daniely--Shalev-Shwartz DS dimension (\Cref{open:formalize-ds}); so the edge is red
and dashed, and the toggle to solid would require formalizing the true DS
dimension and its domination, not the inequality the kernel currently certifies.

\item \textbf{Agnostic drops realizability, yet VC still characterizes it in the
literature.} Read the green $agn \to pac$ restriction edge together with the blue
$vc \to agn$ edge. Together they mark the boundary where the relationship type and the
evidence diverge: agnostic PAC generalizes standard PAC by removing the
realizability constraint (the green \rel{restricts} edge,
\Cref{def:agnostic-pac-ext}), and the characterizing condition is still finite VC
dimension (\Cref{thm:agnostic-ft}); but the kernel has only the \emph{definition}
\leanname{AgnosticPACLearnable}, not an agnostic characterization theorem, so $vc
\to agn$ is dashed while $agn \to pac$ is dotted, and the two edges out of the
agnostic node carry different evidence grades for the same setting.

\item \textbf{Noise adds $\eta$, yet finite VC still suffices.} Read the new $noise
\to pac$ edge that connects the previously floating node. Its color and style sit
on a boundary: noise tolerance extends the grammar with the
noise rate $\eta$ (the orange \rel{extends\_grammar} edge), yet finite VC dimension
plus a \emph{known} $\eta < 1/2$ already suffices for PAC learnability under
classification noise (\Cref{thm:noise-tolerant},
\cite{AngluinLaird1988}); because that theorem carries no kernel handle, the edge
is drawn dotted rather than solid, and statistical-query learnability is the bridge
(\Cref{rmk:sq-noise}).

\item \textbf{Formalizing one characterization turns a dashed edge solid.} Pick the
multiclass or the agnostic characterization and ask what machine-checking it would
change. This is the frontier the figure marks but cannot yet draw solid:
formalizing the genuine DS dimension and \Cref{thm:multiclass-char} would promote
$ds \to pac$ from dashed to solid (\Cref{open:formalize-ds}), and formalizing the
agnostic fundamental theorem would promote $vc \to agn$; each such move grows the
verified subgraph outward from the single $vc \to pac$ rung into the extension
periphery, and the figure's dashed edges are exactly the inventory of which
characterization to climb next.

\item \textbf{The open problems are the dashed and dotted edges read forward.} Read
the periphery edges as a to-do list. This is the frontier the map and the
open-problems section share a vocabulary for: closing the multiclass $\Theta(\log
k)$ gap (\Cref{open:multiclass-tight}), proving pseudodimension characterizes
realizable real-valued learning (\Cref{open:pdim-realizable}), and finding the
fat-shattering analogue of noise tolerance (\Cref{open:noise-fat}) are each a
specific dashed or dotted edge whose evidence grade the corresponding theorem would
raise; so the white space between solid and dashed in the figure is the chapter's
research frontier, and the lone solid edge is a lower bound on what is provable.

\item \textbf{Is grammar growth itself a provable no-single-dimension invariant?}
Read the entire orange backbone as a single claim about the impossibility of one
universal measure. This is the figure's deepest frontier: the chapter proves
grammar growth is \emph{necessary} only in the multiclass case, where the $\Ndim =
1$, $\DSdim = \infty$ class shows no two-function dimension can characterize
learnability when the label set is infinite (\Cref{prop:ds-vs-nat}), while for the
real-valued, agnostic, and noisy settings the necessity remains a working
understanding short of a theorem; so the conjecture that \emph{no single combinatorial dimension
characterizes all extensions at once}, a no-free-lunch for shattering primitives of
which the Natarajan--DS gap is the one proven instance, is the meta-question about
the whole backbone, and because no verifier yet certifies it, this reading of the
map is drawn dotted.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Counterfactual exercises: generalizing Figure~\ref{fig:ds-shattering}}

Figure~\ref{fig:ds-shattering} is a definition drawn twice, and these exercises
read it at both resolutions.  On the left sits a \emph{witness table}: a default
labeling $f$ with one single-coordinate-deviation witness $h_i$ per point
(\Cref{def:ds-dim-ext}).  On the right sits the same data as a \emph{star} in the
one-inclusion hypergraph $\mathrm{OIG}(\mathcal H, S)$ (\Cref{def:oig}), the vertex
$f$ joined to each $h_i$ along the coordinate-$i$ edge.  Each item below widens the
concrete $d=3$ drawing into a general claim, identifying it as an \emph{instance}
(a labeling, a deviation cell, or a coordinate edge already shown), a
\emph{boundary} (one quantifier, direction, or label-set assumption toggled, so the
per-point requirement, the $\Ndim$ versus $\DSdim$ order, or the evidence status
changes), or a \emph{frontier} (a structure or formalization the figure points
toward but does not contain).  One evidence rule governs the whole block: DS-shattering
and the characterization it drives are literature, so no DS object here may be
called machine-checked.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{The $d=3$ table is the smallest faithful instance of the definition.}
Read only the left panel.  Show it is a correct concrete witness for
\Cref{def:ds-dim-ext} with $d=3$: a single default $f$ assigning $a,b,c$, and three
hypotheses $h_1,h_2,h_3$ each agreeing with $f$ on two coordinates and deviating on
the third.  Argue that this is the defining instance of DS-shattering: it exhibits
$\DSdim \ge 3$ for any class containing $\{f,h_1,h_2,h_3\}$, a worked example of the
definition with nothing claimed verified, no theorem behind it.

\item \textbf{The table and the star are one object at two resolutions.}  Read the
left and right panels together.  Show that ``a table of $d$ rows, each deviating
from $f$ at exactly one coordinate'' is literally ``a star of $d$ coordinate-edges
out of the central vertex $f$'' in $\mathrm{OIG}(\mathcal H, S)$ (\Cref{def:oig}),
so the right panel is the instance that makes the table's structural content
visible.  Conclude that generalizing the figure to arbitrary $d$ means drawing a
$d$-edge star; adding table rows is the same operation seen from the left.

\item \textbf{The single-deviation cell is the DS primitive.}  Read one red cell
in isolation, say $h_2$ at $x_2$.  Show that the whole definition is generated by
this one move, deviate at exactly one point and agree elsewhere, iterated over the
$d$ coordinates, and that this is the instance distinguishing DS-shattering from
every all-labelings notion: DS asks for exactly $d$ witnesses, one per coordinate,
where an all-labelings notion would demand one per labeling.

\item \textbf{DS-shattering needs only one witness per point, where Natarajan needs
all $2^d$.}
Replace the figure's three witness rows with the $2^d = 8$ rows Natarajan
shattering would require of a global pair $(f_0,f_1)$ (\Cref{def:natarajan-dim},
\Cref{ex:natarajan-k3}).  This swap exposes the boundary where the per-point requirement
weakens: the figure draws $d$ single flips, whereas N-shattering draws the full
cube of interpolations, so DS-shattering is strictly the weaker demand at each
point yet, by Exercise~5, can hold on larger sets.

\item \textbf{Weaker per point, larger dimension: $\Ndim \le \DSdim$.}  Toggle the
direction of comparison.  Using \Cref{prop:ds-vs-nat}, show every N-shattered set
is DS-shattered (take $f = f_0$ and let $h_i$ realize the basis string $e_i$), so
$\Ndim(\mathcal H) \le \DSdim(\mathcal H)$ always.  Argue this is the boundary the
figure sits on: the per-point weakening (Exercise~4) is exactly what lets the
dimension grow, and the star picture is the witness that DS asks less locally while
encoding more globally.

\item \textbf{The infinite-label obstruction: $\Ndim = 1$, $\DSdim = \infty$.}
Push the previous boundary to its extreme.  Invoking the obstruction box, describe
the Brukhim et al.\ (2022) class with $\Ndim(\mathcal H)=1$ yet
$\DSdim(\mathcal H)=\infty$, where the one-inclusion hypergraph contains
arbitrarily large pseudo-cubes although no three points are N-shattered.  Here lies
the boundary at which the figure's local-looking table must be read globally:
when $|Y|=\infty$, local two-label structure (Natarajan) does not bound the star
structure (DS), and only the latter governs learnability.

\item \textbf{The star is the object the algorithm runs on.}  Read the right panel
as input to a learner.  Using the proof sketch of \Cref{thm:multiclass-char},
explain that finiteness of $\DSdim$, no large pseudo-cube, yields a proper
orientation of $\mathrm{OIG}(\mathcal H, S)$ with outdegree $O(\DSdim)$, and the
learner returns a minimum-outdegree vertex.  Argue the figure's star is therefore
the boundary object between combinatorics (DS-shattering) and the algorithm (the
orientation): the same drawing the learner consumes as input, doing real work
rather than illustrating it, and that this theorem is literature, not
kernel-verified.

\item \textbf{The kernel verifies a different pair, running the other way.}  Read
the provenance note in the caption.  The kernel formalizes \leanname{NatarajanDim}
and the all-labelings \leanname{StrongShatteringDim} and proves
\leanname{strongShatteringDim_le_NatarajanDim}, that is, strong-shattering dimension
below Natarajan.  This separates what is drawn from what is
checked: the verified inequality concerns the \emph{all-labelings} dimension (every
$f\colon S\to Y$ realized) and runs \emph{opposite} to \Cref{prop:ds-vs-nat}'s
$\Ndim\le\DSdim$, so no part of the figure's DS structure is among the
machine-checked objects, and conflating \leanname{StrongShatteringDim} with the
single-deviation DS notion would be an error.

\item \textbf{Formalize the DS dimension (an unknown known).}  Treat the star as a
formalization target.  Following \Cref{open:formalize-ds}, sketch what a kernel
\texttt{DSShatteringDim} would require, the one-inclusion hypergraph, the
default-plus-single-deviation predicate, and the orientation argument, and argue
this is the frontier the figure points to: the picture is complete and correct as a
definition, but no machine-checked theorem stands behind it, and closing
$\Ndim\le\DSdim$ in Lean would be the move that turns this literature illustration
into verified mathematics.

\item \textbf{Is one primitive enough?}  Treat the figure as one of several
multiclass shattering pictures.  Natarajan shattering needs a global pair and all
$2^d$ interpolations; strong shattering needs all labelings; DS-shattering needs a
default and $d$ single flips.  Ask whether a single shattering primitive could
unify all three, so that one figure would replace the chapter's several, or whether
the proliferation is necessary because finite and infinite label sets genuinely
require different objects, the $\Theta(\log k)$ gap of \Cref{open:multiclass-tight}
and the no-single-dimension question raised by Figure~\ref{fig:ch17-concept-map}.
This is the frontier: whether the figure is one of many because the mathematics
demands many, or only because no unifying primitive has yet been found.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Counterfactual exercises: generalizing Figure~\ref{fig:fat-shattering}}

Of the five figures in this chapter, this one alone draws a definition the kernel
has formalized, so the exercises below trade on a sharp split between a verified
definition and a borrowed theorem.  Figure~\ref{fig:fat-shattering} exhibits
\Cref{def:fat-shattering-ext}: a shared threshold vector $(t_1,t_2,t_3)$, a
symmetric forbidden band of width $2\gamma$ centred on each $t_i$, and two function
curves $f_A,f_B$ that realize the labelings $b=101$ and $b=010$ by landing, at every
$x_i$, inside the correct allowed zone ($\geq t_i+\gamma$ or $\leq t_i-\gamma$).
Each item generalizes the concrete $d=3$ drawing, placing it as an \emph{instance}
(a labeling, a point, a band, or a threshold already drawn), a \emph{boundary} (one
quantifier, one scale parameter, or one evidence status toggled, so the margin
requirement, the $\fatshat_\gamma$ versus $\Pdim$ relation, or the verification
status changes), or a \emph{frontier} (a structure or formalization the figure
points toward but does not contain).  Keep the split clean: the configuration
instantiates the kernel-formalized definition \leanname{FatShatteringDim}, while
\Cref{thm:real-valued-char} is literature, so no characterization of real-valued
learnability may be called machine-checked.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{The $d=3$ picture with two curves is the smallest faithful instance of the
definition.}  Show the figure is a correct concrete witness for
\Cref{def:fat-shattering-ext} with $d=3$: a single threshold vector $(t_1,t_2,t_3)$ shared by
both curves, and two functions $f_A,f_B$ that, at each $x_i$, satisfy $f(x_i)\geq t_i+\gamma$
where the label is $1$ and $f(x_i)\leq t_i-\gamma$ where it is $0$.  Verify the six value
dots fall in their zones for $b=101$ and $b=010$, and argue this exhibits
$\fatshat_\gamma(\mathcal F)\geq 3$ for any class containing $f_A,f_B$ and witnesses for the
remaining labelings, with nothing claimed verified beyond the definition itself.

\item \textbf{The curves are what make this an exhibition; the bands alone would not.}  Contrast the
present figure with a version drawing only the thresholds and the $2\gamma$ bands.  Show that
the bands alone depict the \emph{predicate} (the geometry of $f(x_i)\geq t_i+\gamma$ versus
$\leq t_i-\gamma$), whereas the curves depict the \emph{witnesses} the existential in
\Cref{def:fat-shattering-ext} asserts (``there exists $f\in\mathcal F$'').  Conclude that the
defining content of fat-shattering is carried by the functions clearing the margin, exactly as
the witness-exhibition reading of VC-shattering carries the content of the binary definition.

\item \textbf{The $2\gamma$ band is the scale primitive.}  Read one band in isolation, say at
$x_2$.  Show that the symmetric forbidden region $(t_2-\gamma,\,t_2+\gamma)$ is the one object
in the figure with no binary analogue: in classification the outputs are already $0/1$ and no
margin exists.  Argue this band is the grammar extension named in \Cref{def:fat-shattering-ext}
(the new primitive beyond pseudodimension is the margin $\gamma$), and that its symmetry
about $t_i$ is forced by the mirror-image clauses $f(x_i)\geq t_i+\gamma$ and
$f(x_i)\leq t_i-\gamma$.

\item \textbf{The threshold vector is the pre-margin primitive, shared across labelings.}
Read the three dashed lines $t_1,t_2,t_3$ alone.  Show that a \emph{single} threshold vector
must serve \emph{every} labeling: $f_A$ and $f_B$ realize opposite labelings yet are scored
against the same $t_i$ (\Cref{def:fat-shattering-ext}).  Compare with
\Cref{def:pseudodim-ext}, where the identical threshold-vector primitive appears without a
margin, and argue that fat-shattering adds the band $2\gamma$ on top of the pseudodimension's
threshold structure rather than replacing it.

\item \textbf{$\gamma\to 0$ collapses fat-shattering to pseudodimension.}  Toggle the scale
parameter to its limit.  Shrink the $2\gamma$ band to zero width and show the requirement
degenerates from $f(x_i)\geq t_i+\gamma$ or $\leq t_i-\gamma$ to $f(x_i)\geq t_i$ or
$<t_i$, which is precisely the $P$-shattering condition of \Cref{def:pseudodim-ext}.
Conclude that $\fatshat_\gamma(\mathcal F)\to\Pdim(\mathcal F)$ as $\gamma\to 0$, so the figure
in the zero-margin limit becomes the pseudodimension picture, and that both
\leanname{FatShatteringDim} and \leanname{Pseudodimension} are kernel-formalized definitions
with the latter the limit of the former.

\item \textbf{The margin makes fat-shattering strictly stronger than threshold-crossing.}
Push the previous boundary the other way.  Exhibit a class and a value
$\gamma' > 0$ for which some labeling can be realized with values that merely cross each $t_i$
(so the points are $P$-shattered) but cannot be realized while clearing the band by $\gamma'$
on both sides.  This is the boundary at which $\fatshat_{\gamma'}(\mathcal F)$ drops below
$\Pdim(\mathcal F)$: passing the threshold no longer suffices, since the function must land in the light
zone, which is the scale-sensitivity the figure draws.

\item \textbf{Two curves shown, $2^d$ functions required.}  Replace the figure's pair of
curves with the full witness set.  Show that \Cref{def:fat-shattering-ext} demands one function
per labeling, hence $2^d=8$ functions for $d=3$, of which the figure exhibits $b=101$ and
$b=010$; the remaining six (for instance $b=111$, all dots above; $b=000$, all dots below)
must also land in their zones against the \emph{same} $(t_1,t_2,t_3)$.  Argue that generalizing
the figure to arbitrary $d$ means asserting a function for every one of the $2^d$ binary
strings, the witness count growing exponentially while the sample points grow only linearly.

\item \textbf{Larger margins shatter fewer points.}  Toggle $\gamma$ upward.  Using
\Cref{rmk:scale-family}, argue that widening every band (larger $\gamma$) makes the two
allowed zones recede from each threshold, so functions of bounded range can clear the margin at
fewer points; hence $\fatshat_\gamma(\mathcal F)$ is typically a decreasing function of
$\gamma$.  This monotonicity is why the characterizing object is a scale \emph{family}
$\{\fatshat_\gamma\}_{\gamma>0}$, one finiteness requirement per $\gamma$ in place of a single
number, and connect this to the grammar-growth reading of the real-valued setting.

\item \textbf{Fat-shattering is a formalized definition; the characterization it drives is
literature.}  Separate the two evidence grades the figure straddles.  Argue that the drawn
configuration instantiates the kernel-formalized definition \leanname{FatShatteringDim}
($\mathrm{sc}=0$), so it may be named as such, but that \Cref{thm:real-valued-char} (finite
$\fatshat_\gamma$ for all $\gamma$ if and only if agnostic real-valued PAC learnability, due to
Alon, Ben-David, Cesa-Bianchi, and Haussler, 1997) and \Cref{prop:pdim-covering} are
literature results with no kernel proof.  State precisely which claim attaches to the figure (a
definition is exhibited) and which does not (the kernel does not prove real-valued
learnability).

\item \textbf{Frontiers: the zero-margin limit, noise, the tight rate, and one invariant
versus a family.}  Read the figure as pointing past itself.  (a) Pseudodimension is the
$\gamma\to 0$ limit drawn in Exercise~5; does it \emph{characterize} realizable real-valued
learning the way fat-shattering characterizes the agnostic case
(\Cref{open:pdim-realizable})?  (b) What is the fat-shattering analogue of noise tolerance
(\Cref{open:noise-fat})?  (c) Is the covering-number bound that $\fatshat_\gamma$ drives in the
proof sketch of \Cref{thm:real-valued-char} tight, or can the $\log^2$ factor be removed?
(d) Is the scale family $\{\fatshat_\gamma\}_{\gamma>0}$ reducible to a single invariant, or is
the $\gamma$-indexed family irreducible?  Argue the figure is evidence for irreducibility: a
static frame can draw only one $\gamma$, and \Cref{rmk:scale-family} insists the
characterization is a whole family of finiteness conditions, one per scale, beyond what any single number records.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Counterfactual exercises: generalizing Figure~\ref{fig:proper-improper}}

This table earns its keep by separating two things textbooks usually run together:
where proper and improper learning agree, and where they part.  Read
Figure~\ref{fig:proper-improper} as a $2\times2$ lattice with two columns (improper,
proper) and two layers.  The statistical layer is drawn solid because both its
cells are machine-checked; the computational layer is dashed because the separation
there is literature.  The exercises below lift the concrete table into a general
claim, treating it as an \emph{instance} (a cell, relation, or witness already
shown), a \emph{boundary} (one layer, restriction, or evidence grade toggled, so a
verdict or its verification status changes), or a \emph{frontier} (a formalization
or setting the table points toward but does not contain).  The evidence rule never
relaxes: the statistical row is kernel-verified and the computational row is
literature, so the computational gap is never to be called machine-checked.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{The table is the definition.}  Read \Cref{def:proper-improper-ext} directly off
the grid.  The grid is the instance the figure exhibits: the columns encode the restriction
$A(S)\in\mathcal H$ versus $A(S)\in\mathcal H'$, while the rows encode the layer in which that
restriction is evaluated.  Explain why the definition names only one restriction yet the
figure needs two layers: the restriction is fixed, but the verdict it produces is read once
per layer.

\item \textbf{The statistical no-gap row is doubly machine-checked.}  Read the top row, both
cells solid.  This row is the instance that proves the absence of a
sample-complexity separation outright, with two checked cells rather than a bare claim: the improper cell is the fundamental theorem that PAC
learnability is exactly finite $\VC$ (\leanname{fundamental_theorem}), and the proper cell is a
verified construction of a proper learner from finite $\VC$, one that outputs a hypothesis in
the class with bounded error (\leanname{vcdim_finite_imp_proper_finite_support_learner}); since
both cells carry $\Theta(d/\varepsilon)$ and both are kernel-verified, the equality
stands as a theorem with a machine-checked proof behind each half.

\item \textbf{One witness threads all four cells.}  Trace $3$-term DNF through the grid.  This
single class is the instance that inhabits the abstract lattice: its status is
constant along the statistical row (learnable both ways) and splits along the computational
row, NP-hard properly yet polynomial-time improperly as $3$-CNF (the separation box and
\Cref{thm:pitt-valiant}).

\item \textbf{The layer toggle is what produces the gap.}  Hold the columns fixed and
intervene on the row.  Define $\mathrm{gap}(\text{layer})$ as the worst-case proper-to-improper
cost ratio in that layer, and read off the boundary the figure draws:
$\mathrm{gap}(\text{statistical})=\Theta(1)$, forced by the two verified cells of Exercise~2,
while $\mathrm{gap}(\text{computational})$ is superpolynomial unless $\mathrm{RP}=\mathrm{NP}$.
The gap is an effect of the layer: fixing the learner type and toggling only the
row is what turns a $\Theta(1)$ ratio into a superpolynomial one.

\item \textbf{Proper is improper restricted to the class.}  Read the dashed
$\subseteq\mathcal H$ relation between the columns.  This relation marks the boundary where a pure
restriction of the hypothesis space (\Cref{def:proper-improper-ext}) leaves one quantity
invariant and blows up another: the $\VC$ dimension of $\mathcal H$ is preserved, so the
sample complexity is unchanged, but the cost of \emph{searching} $\mathcal H$ for a consistent
member is not, which is exactly why the restriction is free in the top row and expensive in
the bottom.

\item \textbf{Qualitative learnability versus the tight rate.}  Read the proper statistical
cell carefully.  This cell sits on the boundary between what the kernel verifies and what it
sharpens: \leanname{vcdim_finite_imp_proper_finite_support_learner} delivers a proper learner
with error at most $1/3$ on bounded support, which already places the proper cell in the same
row as the improper one (proper learnability with no asymptotic sample blow-up), while the
exact matching $\Theta(d/\varepsilon)$ bound remains a literature refinement
(\cite{KearnsVazirani1994}) that the kernel does not yet prove.

\item \textbf{What the kernel sees, and what it does not.}  Read the solid top row against the
dashed bottom row.  The two border styles encode a boundary: the
design-lab kernel reasons about sample complexity and statistical learnability and proves the
whole top row, but exposes no handle for NP-hardness, so no cell of the bottom row is verified.
Conclude that, for this topic, the verified-versus-literature frontier coincides exactly with
the statistical-versus-computational frontier.

\item \textbf{Formalize the NP-hardness.}  Treat the bottom-right cell as a formalization
target.  The Pitt and Valiant reduction from graph $k$-colouring (\Cref{thm:pitt-valiant},
\cite{PittValiant1988}) is literature; sketch what a machine-checked version would require, a
kernel encoding of $k$-colouring, of the labelled-sample construction, and of the equivalence
between a consistent $k$-term DNF and a proper $k$-colouring.  This is the frontier that
would turn the bottom-right cell solid while the rest of the bottom row stayed literature.

\item \textbf{The partial-concept failure of ERM.}  Add a third row.  In the partial-concept
model (\Cref{def:partial-concept}, \cite{AlonHannekeHolzmanMoran2021}) there are classes of
partial $\VC$ dimension $1$ with no finite-$\VC$ total extension that empirical risk
minimization cannot learn at all.  This is the frontier where the proper and ERM route
fails \emph{statistically} rather than computationally: unlike the computational row, the
proper cell of this third row is empty (no learner) even though improper learning still
succeeds, so the figure's two-layer picture is itself a special case of a taller table.

\item \textbf{An invariance theorem for the gap.}  Conjecture that no statistical dimension
detects the proper-improper gap: for every sample-complexity measure, the VC, Natarajan, and
fat-shattering dimensions among them, the proper and improper cells agree.  This conjecture is the
figure's deepest frontier, an invariance statement that the gap is invariant-zero under every
statistical measurement and nonzero only under a runtime measurement; identify the already
verified facts (\leanname{fundamental_theorem} on the improper side,
\leanname{vcdim_finite_imp_proper_finite_support_learner} on the proper side) that would be the
base case of such a theorem, and argue that no static frame can certify the universal claim,
so this reading of the table is itself drawn dashed.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Counterfactual exercises: generalizing Figure~\ref{fig:grammar-growth}}

This hub-and-spoke is the chapter's thesis drawn as a single picture: one verified
center, four irreducible extensions radiating out.  The hub is binary PAC learning,
the single claim the kernel certifies sorry-free (finite VC characterizes realizable
binary PAC, \leanname{fundamental_theorem}).  Each wavy orange arrow is an
\rel{extends\_grammar} edge (\Cref{sec:extends-grammar}) that grows the binary theory
by adding one primitive with no binary analogue, and each corner box names the
extended setting and its characterizing dimension.  The figure's whole content is
that five different extensions are instances of one move, adding irreducible
vocabulary, where a naive reading would file them as five unrelated facts.  Each
exercise below widens the concrete hub-and-spoke into a general claim, identifying it
as an \emph{instance} (a box, an arrow, or a primitive already drawn), a
\emph{boundary} (one label, direction, or evidence grade toggled so an edge changes
meaning, kind, or status), or a \emph{frontier} (a structure or formalization the
figure points toward but does not contain).  The evidence rule holds throughout: the
hub is verified, the extension dimensions are formalized definitions, and the
characterizations and the irreducibility claim are literature, with the multiclass
case the one proven instance.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{The hub is the chapter's one verified rung.} Read only the center
box and ignore the four spokes.  The hub is the figure's defining verified
instance: of every claim in the diagram, exactly one is machine-checked, the
biconditional that finite VC dimension characterizes realizable binary PAC
learnability (\leanname{fundamental_theorem}, over $\mathcal{H} \subseteq
\{0,1\}^X$); the four corner characterizations are borrowed from the literature,
so the verified part of the figure is the hub alone and every spoke grows outward
from it, which is why the heavier border marks the hub as the lower bound on what
the kernel currently proves.

\item \textbf{Five extensions are instances of one move.} Read the four orange
arrows as a single class and ignore which box each one reaches.  This orange class is the
instance that names the chapter's thesis (\Cref{sec:extends-grammar}): each arrow
is one \rel{extends\_grammar} edge that adds a primitive absent from the binary
language, so the question ``how does binary PAC generalize?'' has the structural
answer ``by adding irreducible vocabulary,'' and the hub-and-spoke is the witness
that the multiclass, real-valued, agnostic, and noisy extensions are four instances of
one relation, where they might have looked like four separate constructions.

\item \textbf{Each spoke carries exactly one new primitive.} Pick any single arrow
and read its label against its box.  Each arrow is the instance that exhibits its own
per-extension primitive: the multiclass arrow adds the one-inclusion witness for
the DS dimension (\Cref{def:ds-dim-ext}), the real-valued arrow adds a threshold
vector and then the scale $\gamma$ for the pseudo- and fat-shattering dimensions
(\Cref{def:pseudodim-ext}, \Cref{def:fat-shattering-ext}), the agnostic arrow adds
the relative benchmark, and the noise arrow adds the rate $\eta$
(\Cref{thm:noise-tolerant}); each is one expansion of the binary grammar, and the
figure asserts these expansions are the same kind of move.

\item \textbf{The corrected multiclass spoke is isomorphic to the DS witness
picture.} Read the multiclass arrow's primitive, the one-inclusion witness, and
compare it to Figure~\ref{fig:ds-shattering}.  This is the instance where the
spoke and the dimension it produces speak the same language: the one-inclusion
witness is precisely the single-coordinate-deviation structure of the DS dimension
(\Cref{def:ds-dim-ext}), the star in the one-inclusion hypergraph
$\mathrm{OIG}(\mathcal{H}, S)$ (\Cref{def:oig}); so the arrow that yields the
$\DSdim$ box is labeled with the very object that defines $\DSdim$, and the
multiclass spoke is a compressed pointer to the witness figure.

\item \textbf{An \rel{extends\_grammar} edge resists redrawing as a \rel{restricts}
edge.} Read any orange arrow and ask whether it could be drawn as a special-case
inclusion instead.  Here is the figure's defining boundary: a \rel{restricts} edge
would mean the extension is a special case of binary PAC needing no new
vocabulary, whereas every arrow here marks a place where reduction to the binary
theory loses information (the non-reduction principle, \Cref{rmk:non-reduction}),
so toggling any arrow from \rel{extends\_grammar} to \rel{restricts} would falsely
claim the new primitive is dispensable, and the figure's wavy style is exactly the
refusal to make that claim.

\item \textbf{The multiclass box is $\DSdim$, so its primitive is the one-inclusion
witness, where two-function shattering belongs to Natarajan.} Read the top-left box and its arrow together and ask whether the
primitive matches the dimension.  Here is the boundary the corrected label
protects: the box is characterized by the DS dimension, whose primitive is the
one-inclusion witness, while two-function shattering is the Natarajan primitive
(\Cref{def:natarajan-dim}, \cite{Natarajan1989}), and the two are not
interchangeable because a class can have Natarajan dimension one and infinite DS
dimension (\Cref{prop:ds-vs-nat}); so labeling this arrow ``two-function
shattering'' would contradict its own box and the chapter's resolution that DS is
the dimension characterizing multiclass learnability, the role Natarajan cannot fill (\Cref{thm:multiclass-char},
\cite{BrukhimCarmonDinurMoranYehudayoff2022}).

\item \textbf{Agnostic keeps the same condition yet is still a grammar extension.}
Read the bottom-left box, whose condition is finite VC, the same as the hub.  Here
is the boundary where the finiteness condition is unchanged while the edge stays
\rel{extends\_grammar}: agnostic PAC drops realizability and measures success
relative to the best in class (\Cref{thm:agnostic-ft}, \cite{Haussler1992}), so the
new vocabulary is the relative benchmark $\inf_{h \in \mathcal{H}} \risk(h)$ in
place of a new dimension; the arrow therefore adds vocabulary without changing the
finiteness condition, which shows generalization here is a matter of language while
the characterizing quantity holds fixed.

\item \textbf{The dimensions are formalized, but their characterizations are not.}
Read the corner boxes as naming dimensions and ask which parts the kernel
certifies.  This is the figure's principal evidence boundary: the
pseudodimension, the fat-shattering dimension, and the all-labelings strong
shattering dimension are machine-checked structures (\leanname{Pseudodimension},
\leanname{FatShatteringDim}, \leanname{StrongShatteringDim}, with the verified
inequality \leanname{strongShatteringDim_le_NatarajanDim}), yet the theorems that
make a dimension \emph{characterize} a setting, in the corner boxes, are literature
(\Cref{thm:real-valued-char}, \cite{AlonBenDavidCesaBianchiHaussler1997}); so a
dimension can be a verified definition while its characterizing claim stays
borrowed, and drawing the boxes as verified because the dimensions are formalized
would overstate what the kernel proves.

\item \textbf{Formalizing one spoke's dimension is the next move.} Pick the
multiclass spoke and ask what machine-checking it would change.  This is the
frontier the figure marks but cannot yet draw verified: the $\DSdim$ box names a
literature dimension, because the kernel's \leanname{StrongShatteringDim} is the
all-labelings dimension and runs below \leanname{NatarajanDim}, the \emph{opposite}
of the true $\Ndim \le \DSdim$, so formalizing the genuine DS dimension and
\Cref{thm:multiclass-char} would turn the multiclass spoke from literature into
kernel-anchored (\Cref{open:formalize-ds}); the same move for the real-valued,
agnostic, and noise spokes is open, and the spokes are the inventory of which
characterization to climb next.

\item \textbf{Is grammar growth itself a provable no-single-dimension invariant?}
Read the entire orange backbone as one claim about the impossibility of a single
universal measure.  This is the figure's deepest frontier and the chapter's
synthesis: grammar growth is proved \emph{necessary} only for multiclass, where the
$\Ndim = 1$, $\DSdim = \infty$ class shows no two-function dimension can
characterize learnability when the label set is infinite (\Cref{prop:ds-vs-nat}),
while for the real-valued, agnostic, and noisy spokes the necessity is the working
understanding rather than a theorem; so the conjecture that \emph{no single
combinatorial dimension characterizes all four extensions at once}, a
no-free-lunch for shattering primitives of which the Natarajan--DS gap is the one
proven instance, is the meta-question the whole hub-and-spoke raises, the same
no-single-dimension reading that the concept map leaves dotted
(\Cref{fig:ch17-concept-map}), and because no verifier yet certifies it, this
reading of the figure is the open invariant the chapter closes on.
\end{enumerate}

\bigskip

This chapter has traced the grammar growth required to extend binary PAC
learning theory to multiclass, real-valued, agnostic, noise-tolerant, and
computationally constrained settings.  Each extension introduces irreducible
new primitives and yields its own characterization theorem or separation
result.  The next chapter examines what lies beyond these
characterizations: the application-layer concepts, the open problems, and the
scope boundaries of the theory.
